Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 November 2026 · Kiln 4.1 for iPhone, iPad, and Mac

Kiln does its work on your device. There's no account, no Kiln server and no analytics, and Kiln never sends your recordings, transcripts or reports anywhere. Here's what that means in practice.

Your content stays on your device

Your recordings, transcripts, speaker names and reports are stored inside Kiln on your device. Kiln never uploads any of them.

They can leave your device only in these ways, and each one is up to you:

  • You share them. When you share a recording or a report, or save it as a PDF, it goes wherever you send it.
  • You copy them. Text you copy can be pasted into any app. With Handoff turned on, you can also paste it on your other Apple devices.
  • Your device backs up. Kiln's data is included in your iCloud or computer backups, like other apps' data. Those backups belong to you (and to Apple, if you use iCloud), and we never see them. A backup on a computer is protected with a password only if you turn on encryption for it.

While you record on iPhone or iPad, the meeting's title appears on your Lock Screen, and in the Dynamic Island on iPhone. Meeting reminders show the event's title too, and like other notifications they can appear on your Lock Screen and Apple Watch. All of this happens on your device.

No account, no analytics

Kiln doesn't ask you to sign in, and it has no server of its own. There's no analytics, tracking or advertising in the app, and nothing in it collects data about you.

Microphone

Kiln uses the microphone only to record, and keeps recording when you switch apps or lock your screen. If you'd rather not give microphone access, you can import an audio file instead and use everything else.

Everything happens on your device

Transcription, telling speakers apart, meeting titles, speaker-name suggestions and reports all happen on your device. None of it uses a server, ours or anyone else's.

Transcription uses Apple's speech recognition. If your device doesn't already have a language, it downloads it from Apple the first time you use it, and looks after it from then on. Everything Kiln needs to tell speakers apart comes built into the app.

Reports, titles and speaker-name suggestions use Apple Intelligence on your device. In Kiln, Settings → Apple Intelligence shows whether it's ready on your device.

Internet connection

Kiln for Mac never connects to the internet itself: it's built without permission to go online.

On iPhone and iPad, Kiln connects to the internet for one thing only: asking the App Store whether a newer version of Kiln is available. It sends nothing about you or your meetings, and it goes only to Apple. Like any internet connection, it lets Apple see your device's internet address (its IP address), which can show roughly where you are. If an update exists, Kiln mentions it in Settings and in a banner you can dismiss. Updates are never forced.

Calendar and reminders (optional)

Meeting reminders are off until you turn them on. Then Kiln asks to see your calendar and to send notifications, and reminds you shortly before each meeting. To spot meetings, it looks at each event's title, time, how many people are invited, and its link, location and notes, to find a video-call link. It also lists your calendars so you can choose which ones to watch. Apple's calendar permission only comes as full access, but Kiln only reads your events. It never changes them and never sends them anywhere.

Contacting support (optional)

When you use Report an Issue (Settings → Help & Feedback), Kiln prepares an email in your own mail app. Nothing is sent until you send it. Two things are included unless you turn them off:

  • Device info — your Kiln version, device model, system version and memory.
  • Diagnostics — a short log of what the app did, such as which step ran and how long the transcript was, along with your Kiln version and device details, plus Apple's crash reports. The log never includes your recordings, transcripts, meeting titles, speaker names or reports, and Kiln adds nothing of yours to Apple's crash reports. You can preview the log before you send it. If your device has no mail account set up, Kiln opens a plain email draft instead, and the diagnostics can't be attached.

We use what you send only to help with your issue. Our support email is hosted by Gmail, so Google stores those messages. We keep them only as long as we need them to help you, and we'll delete them whenever you ask.

Deleting your data

Deleting a meeting deletes its recording, transcript, speaker names and every report made from it. Settings → Storage → Delete All Meetings removes them all at once. Kiln can't undo a deletion, but a backup made earlier still has the meeting until that backup is replaced. Copies made for sharing are cleared the next time you open Kiln.

The diagnostic log and crash reports stay when you delete meetings. They're small and contain none of your recordings, transcripts, titles, names or reports. Kiln never sends them: only you can, by attaching them to a support email. Like the rest of Kiln's data, they're included in your backups.

To remove everything:

  • iPhone and iPad — delete the app. A backup made earlier keeps its copy until it's replaced.
  • Mac — moving Kiln to the Trash leaves its data behind. Also delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.tushargupta.kiln.

Children's privacy

The Kiln app collects nothing from anyone, including children. There's no account, and the app can't send us anything on its own.
