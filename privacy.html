Privacy Policy

Last updated 5 November 2026 · Kiln 4.1 for iPhone, iPad, and Mac

Kiln does its work on your device. It has no backend of its own, no account, and no analytics or tracking code, and it never sends your recordings, transcripts, or reports anywhere. The rest of this page is the detail behind that sentence, including the few places where something does touch the network.

Your content stays on your device

Audio recordings and imported files are stored in Kiln's own storage inside the app's sandbox; transcripts, speaker records, and generated reports live in its database alongside them. Kiln never uploads any of it. Its only network request is the iPhone and iPad version check described under Network access, and that carries nothing of yours.

Three things can move that content off the device, and all three are yours:

  • You share or export it. Kiln has Share and Export for recordings, and share or PDF export for reports. Once you AirDrop, mail, or upload a file yourself, where it goes is between you and whatever you sent it to.
  • You copy it to the clipboard. Copy Transcript and a report's Copy button put that text on the system clipboard, where any app you paste into can read it. With Handoff turned on, Universal Clipboard can also make it available on your other nearby Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account.
  • Your device backs itself up. Kiln's files sit in ordinary app storage and are not excluded from backup, so they are included in your device's backups like other app data: iCloud Backup or a computer backup on iPhone and iPad, and whatever backup you run on a Mac. A computer backup is encrypted only if you have turned on “Encrypt local backup”. Those backups are handled by Apple and by you. They never come to us, and we cannot see them.

While you record on iPhone or iPad, the meeting's title appears in a Live Activity on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. Meeting reminders show the event's title too, including on the Lock Screen if your notification previews are set to Always. Your device may also show these on a paired Apple Watch. They are created on your device and never pass through us.

No account, no backend, no analytics

Kiln requires no sign-in and has no server of its own. It contains no analytics, tracking, or advertising code, and no third-party SDK that collects data about you or what you do in the app.

Microphone and recording

Kiln asks for microphone access so it can record, and recording continues when you switch away from the app or lock the screen — the app declares background audio for exactly that reason. If you would rather not grant microphone access, you can import an existing audio file instead and use everything else.

You will also see system permission alerts along the way — the microphone one, for example, and the calendar and notification ones if you turn on meeting reminders. Whichever alerts your device shows, none of them changes where your audio goes: Kiln transcribes with Apple's on-device speech models, so your audio and its transcript are processed on your device and are not sent to a speech service.

Network access

Kiln for Mac never connects to the internet itself: it's built without permission to go online. When you use a language that isn't on your Mac yet, macOS downloads Apple's speech model for it from Apple (see Model downloads below).

On iPhone and iPad, Kiln connects to the internet for one thing only: asking the App Store whether a newer version of Kiln is available. It sends nothing about you or your meetings, and it goes only to Apple. Like any internet connection, it shows Apple your IP address. If an update exists, Kiln mentions it in Settings and in a banner you can dismiss. Updates are never forced. As on a Mac, the system downloads Apple's speech model the first time you use a language.

All processing is local

Transcription, speaker separation, meeting titles, speaker-name suggestions, and report generation all run entirely on your device. Transcription, titles, speaker-name suggestions, and reports use Apple's own on-device frameworks; speaker separation uses FluidAudio, an open-source package whose Core ML models ship inside the app. Nothing in that list involves a server, and your content is never sent to us or to any third party for processing.

Model downloads

Kiln transcribes using Apple's on-device speech models, and each transcription language has its own model. The first time you use a language, the system downloads that language's model through Apple's own asset service. Kiln never fetches a model itself and never contacts a non-Apple host; these transfers carry no content of yours and go only to Apple.

Those language models belong to your device rather than to Kiln — they are shared with the rest of the system, and the system manages them. It decides when to keep or remove one, and Kiln has no way to delete one on your behalf, which is why the Languages screen offers no delete control.

The speaker-separation models are bundled inside the app, so nothing is downloaded for them. Report generation uses your device's own Apple Intelligence model: Kiln only checks whether it is available, and never downloads, ships, or replaces it. Whether a device qualifies for Apple Intelligence is Apple's decision, not ours — Settings → Apple Intelligence on your device tells you, and Apple's own support pages list the requirements. On-device transcription has a hardware requirement of its own; where a device cannot run it, Kiln says so plainly on the Languages screen instead of failing quietly.

Calendar and reminders (optional)

Meeting reminders are off until you switch them on. When you do, Kiln asks for calendar access and for permission to send notifications, then reads your upcoming events on this device to schedule local reminders ahead of those meetings. To tell meetings from other events, it looks at each event's title, time, number of attendees, and its link, location and notes (to spot a video-call link). Apple's calendar permission has no read-only option, so Kiln asks for full access, but it only reads: it never creates, edits or deletes an event, and it never sends your events anywhere.

Contacting support (optional)

If you use Report an Issue (Settings → Help & Feedback → Report an Issue) or email us, the message opens in a mail composer using your own email account — or as a draft in your mail app if no account is set up, in which case the attachments can't come along. Nothing is sent automatically: you review it and send it yourself. Two attachment options are switched on by default, and you can turn either off before sending:

  • Device info — app version, hardware model identifier, OS version, and memory size, added to the body of the email.
  • Diagnostics — a small breadcrumb log, plus Apple's crash and performance reports (MetricKit payloads). The log holds only technical details: timestamps, app version, device model, OS version, memory size, thermal state, which processing stages ran and how they ended, counts, the transcript's length as a character count, the language code, the report type, when the rating prompt was shown or dismissed, and error types and codes. Apple's payloads hold call stacks, performance figures and basic device details such as model, OS version and region format. Apple designs them not to include personal data, and Kiln adds nothing of yours to them. Neither contains your audio, transcripts, meeting titles, speaker names, or report content.

You can preview the diagnostic log in the app before you send it. We use whatever you send solely to investigate your issue. Support email is hosted by Gmail (Google), so messages you send us are stored by Google under its privacy policy. We keep a support conversation only as long as we need it to help you, and we'll delete it whenever you ask at the address below.

Deletion

Deleting a meeting deletes its audio recording, its transcript, its speaker records, and every report generated from it. Settings → Storage → Delete All Meetings… removes every meeting the same way, in one step. Kiln can't undo a deletion, though a backup taken before you deleted a meeting still holds it until that backup is replaced or deleted. Copies Kiln made for sharing are temporary and are cleared the next time you open Kiln.

The diagnostic log and Apple's crash and performance reports sit outside your meetings, so neither of those actions removes them. The log is trimmed to its most recent 64 KB each time Kiln starts, and only the 24 most recent crash and performance reports are kept. Neither contains content of yours. Kiln never sends them anywhere: only you can, by attaching them to a support email. Like other app data, they are included in your device's backups.

Uninstalling works differently on each platform:

  • iPhone and iPad — deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device, including the diagnostic files. Copies inside a backup you have already taken stay in that backup until it is replaced or deleted.
  • Mac — dragging Kiln to the Trash removes the app but not its data. Kiln for Mac is sandboxed, so its recordings, database, and diagnostic files live in ~/Library/Containers/com.tushargupta.kiln/Data, and macOS keeps that folder behind. To remove everything, delete that folder as well.

Children's privacy

The Kiln app collects nothing from anyone, including children. There is no account, no sign-in, and no way for the app to send us anything by itself. Kiln never sends what it produces anywhere. Only you can move it, by sharing, copying or emailing it, or through your device's backups.

Contact

The Kiln app collects no personal data, so we have no app data to sell or share. If you email us, we use your message and anything you attach only to respond to you and investigate your issue, and we never sell or share it. If you have any questions about this policy, email support.kiln@gmail.com.
